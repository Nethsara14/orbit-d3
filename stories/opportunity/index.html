Legacy

Opportunity showed that ancient Mars had wet places with neutral-pH water—conditions that could have supported microbial life billions of years ago. It also proved that a small, solar-powered robot can keep working on another planet for nearly 15 Earth years (5,111 Martian sols), far beyond its original 90-sol plan.

Today, Opportunity rests silently on the slopes of Perseverance Valley, along the western rim of Endeavour Crater, after traveling a record 45.16 kilometers (28.06 miles) across the Martian surface. Though its mission ended in the 2018 global dust storm, the engineering lessons and water discoveries it left behind directly shaped later NASA rovers—such as Curiosity and Perseverance—which continue searching for signs of ancient life on Mars today.
